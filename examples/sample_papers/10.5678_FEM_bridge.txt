Finite Element Analysis of Structural Dynamics in Bridge Engineering

Abstract
This study presents a comprehensive finite element analysis of a suspension bridge under dynamic loading conditions. The effects of wind load and traffic load on structural response are investigated.

1. Introduction
Bridge engineering requires accurate prediction of structural behavior under various loading conditions. Finite element methods provide a powerful tool for analyzing complex structures.

2. Methods

2.1 Governing Equations
The equation of motion for structural dynamics is:

$$M\ddot{u} + C\dot{u} + Ku = F(t)$$

where $M$ is the mass matrix (kg), $C$ is the damping matrix (N·s/m), $K$ is the stiffness matrix (N/m), $u$ is the displacement vector (m), $\dot{u}$ is velocity (m/s), $\ddot{u}$ is acceleration (m/s²), and $F(t)$ is the external force vector (N).

The strain-displacement relationship:

$$\epsilon = \frac{\partial u}{\partial x}$$

where $\epsilon$ is the strain (dimensionless).

The constitutive equation (Hooke's law):

$$\sigma = E\epsilon$$

where $\sigma$ is the stress (Pa) and $E$ is the Young's modulus (Pa).

2.2 Numerical Model
The bridge structure is modeled using:
- Total nodes: 15,432
- Total elements: 8,765
- Element type: 8-node hexahedral elements (SOLID45)
- Mesh size: 0.5 m (average)

Material properties:
- Steel: $E = 200$ GPa, $\rho = 7850$ kg/m³, $\nu = 0.3$ (Poisson's ratio)
- Concrete: $E = 30$ GPa, $\rho = 2400$ kg/m³, $\nu = 0.2$

2.3 Boundary Conditions and Loads
Fixed support at bridge piers:
- Displacement: $u = v = w = 0$ m
- Rotation: $\theta_x = \theta_y = \theta_z = 0$ rad

Roller support at expansion joints:
- Vertical displacement: $v = 0$ m
- Horizontal displacement: free
- Rotation: free

Applied loads:
- Dead load: $q_d = 10$ kN/m² (self-weight)
- Live load: $q_l = 15$ kN/m² (traffic)
- Wind load: $q_w = 2.5$ kPa (lateral pressure)

Initial conditions:
- At $t = 0$: $u(x,0) = 0$ m, $\dot{u}(x,0) = 0$ m/s

2.4 Solution Method
The finite element method is employed with:
- Spatial discretization: Galerkin method
- Shape functions: Lagrangian polynomials
- Numerical integration: Gauss quadrature (2×2×2 points)
- Assembly: Global stiffness matrix method

Time integration using Newmark-beta method:
- Time step: $\Delta t = 0.01$ s
- Parameters: $\beta = 0.25$, $\gamma = 0.5$ (implicit scheme)

Solver settings:
- Solver type: Sparse direct solver (PARDISO)
- Convergence criteria: $||R|| < 10^{-5}$ where $R$ is the residual force
- Maximum iterations: 100 per time step
- Convergence norm: L2 norm
