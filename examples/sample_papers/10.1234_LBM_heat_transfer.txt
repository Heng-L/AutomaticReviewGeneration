Computational Study of Heat Transfer in a Channel Flow Using Lattice Boltzmann Method

Abstract
This paper presents a numerical investigation of heat transfer in a two-dimensional channel flow using the Lattice Boltzmann Method (LBM). The effects of Reynolds number and Prandtl number on the heat transfer characteristics are examined.

1. Introduction
Heat transfer in channel flows is a fundamental problem in fluid mechanics and thermal engineering. The Lattice Boltzmann Method has emerged as a powerful tool for simulating fluid flow and heat transfer due to its computational efficiency and ability to handle complex geometries.

2. Numerical Method

2.1 Governing Equations
The lattice Boltzmann equation for fluid flow is given by:

$$f_i(x + e_i \Delta t, t + \Delta t) - f_i(x,t) = -\frac{1}{\tau} [f_i(x,t) - f_i^{eq}(x,t)]$$

where $f_i$ is the distribution function, $e_i$ is the discrete velocity (m/s), $\Delta t$ is the time step (s), and $\tau$ is the relaxation time (dimensionless).

The macroscopic density $\rho$ (kg/m³) and velocity $u$ (m/s) are obtained from:

$$\rho = \sum_i f_i$$

$$\rho u = \sum_i f_i e_i$$

For thermal LBM, the temperature distribution function evolves according to:

$$g_i(x + e_i \Delta t, t + \Delta t) - g_i(x,t) = -\frac{1}{\tau_T} [g_i(x,t) - g_i^{eq}(x,t)]$$

where $g_i$ is the thermal distribution function, $\tau_T$ is the thermal relaxation time, and temperature $T$ (K) is:

$$T = \sum_i g_i$$

2.2 Model Setup
The computational domain consists of a 2D channel with length $L = 0.1$ m and height $H = 0.01$ m. A D2Q9 lattice structure is employed with 9 discrete velocities. The lattice spacing is $\Delta x = 0.0001$ m and time step is $\Delta t = 0.00001$ s.

The Reynolds number is defined as:

$$Re = \frac{U_0 H}{\nu}$$

where $U_0$ is the inlet velocity (m/s), $H$ is the channel height (m), and $\nu$ is the kinematic viscosity (m²/s).

The Prandtl number is:

$$Pr = \frac{\nu}{\alpha}$$

where $\alpha$ is the thermal diffusivity (m²/s).

2.3 Boundary and Initial Conditions
At the inlet ($x = 0$), velocity boundary condition is prescribed:
- $u = U_0 = 0.1$ m/s (constant)
- Temperature: $T = T_h = 373$ K (hot wall)

At the outlet ($x = L$), zero-gradient boundary conditions are applied:
- $\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} = 0$
- $\frac{\partial T}{\partial x} = 0$

At the top and bottom walls ($y = 0$ and $y = H$):
- No-slip condition: $u = 0$ m/s
- Bottom wall: $T = T_c = 293$ K (cold wall)
- Top wall: adiabatic condition $\frac{\partial T}{\partial y} = 0$

Initial conditions:
- Velocity: $u(x,y,0) = 0$ m/s everywhere
- Temperature: $T(x,y,0) = T_c = 293$ K everywhere

2.4 Discretization and Solver
The Lattice Boltzmann Method inherently uses:
- Explicit time integration scheme
- Second-order accurate in space and time
- Collision-streaming algorithm
- BGK (Bhatnagar-Gross-Krook) approximation for collision operator

The convergence criterion is:

$$\frac{||u^{n+1} - u^n||}{||u^{n+1}||} < 10^{-6}$$

where $n$ is the iteration number.

Maximum iterations: 1,000,000
Under-relaxation factor: $\omega = 1.0$ (no relaxation for LBM)
